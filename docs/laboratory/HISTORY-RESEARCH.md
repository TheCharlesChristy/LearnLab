# Great Fire pilot: source and design ledger

Reviewed 29 September 2026. The pilot covers source reasoning about the September 1666 fire, not a definitive reconstruction of its ignition. All in-app evidence cards are newly written **paraphrases** with visible provenance and limits. No source image or quotation is reproduced. The schematic map is an original, deliberately non-scale teaching sketch.

The retained initial plan used a coarse `primary-source` access label for the London Museum overview and a teaching site. This source-specific review corrects that classification: the museum page is modern secondary synthesis, and teaching guides are pedagogical guidance. The archival **records** linked below are primary material; The National Archives' introduction is modern curation.

| Source | Class and use | Important limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [The National Archives, Great Fire collection](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/great-fire-of-london-examine-the-evidence/) | Modern archival curation; dates, context, uncertainty over ignition, competing claims | Its introduction is a modern synthesis, not a 1666 witness. It notes omitted servant voices and incomplete death records. |
| [Pepys diary extracts](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/great-fire-of-london-examine-the-evidence/extracts-from-samuel-pepys-diary/) | 1666 first-person account: an initial alert from a servant, later observations from the river and Bankside, wind and demolition request | Pepys did not witness the first ignition. The alert and house count were reported to him. |
| [London Gazette, 2 September](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/great-fire-of-london-examine-the-evidence/london-gazette-reports-start-of-great-fire/) | Contemporary licensed official news: location near Thames Street/London Bridge; royal response | Its purpose includes presenting the Crown's actions; a report is not direct evidence of ignition mechanism. |
| [Evelyn diary extracts](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/great-fire-of-london-examine-the-evidence/extracts-from-john-evelyns-diary/) | 1666 first-person view from Bankside on 3 September, describing broad extent and easterly wind | A vantage across the river cannot identify the first spark or a precise street-by-street route. |
| [Whitehall account, 8 September](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/great-fire-of-london-examine-the-evidence/a-true-relation-of-the-sad-and-deplorable-fire/) | Contemporary official retrospective report on close wooden buildings, wind and demolition efforts | Its stated defence of government limits its neutrality; claims require comparison. |
| [Hollar's survey, 1667](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/great-fire-of-london-examine-the-evidence/hollars-map-of-the-ruined-city/) and [commission](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/great-fire-of-london-examine-the-evidence/hollars-commission-for-a-map-of-london-ruined-by-fire/) | Primary material about surveyed post-fire ruins and the commission to map them | Not a time-stamped map of the fire's daily movement. The original visual is linked, not copied. |
| [Maynard letter, 9 September](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/great-fire-of-london-examine-the-evidence/fire-caused-by-treachery-or-chance/) | Contemporary correspondence illustrating uncertainty and unverified reports at a distance | Maynard wrote from Eton, not from the ignition site; recounts rumours. |
| [Destruction by a Consuming Fire](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/great-fire-of-london-examine-the-evidence/destruction-by-a-consuming-fire/) | Contemporary pamphlet illustrating rumours that accused French and Dutch people | Accusations are **claims**, not established causes; dates and identities conflict with other records. |
| [London Museum overview](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/great-fire-of-london/) | Modern secondary synthesis for cross-checking setting and multi-causal spread | Not an eyewitness account; do not upgrade its synthesis to primary testimony. |
| [Library of Congress source-analysis guide](https://www.loc.gov/programs/teachers/getting-started-with-primary-sources/guides/) | Official pedagogical guide for observe, reflect, question, creator/purpose and corroboration | A design guide, not evidence that this pilot improves learning outcomes. |

Design decisions: learners inspect sources in any order, then pin two and make a qualified claim. A saved free-text claim is **not automatically scored**; an authored review rubric supports self-check and a human must judge source use, nuance and accuracy. Objective choices test bounded method distinctions, never one preferred historical interpretation. Early evidence creates questions; later sources complicate the account. The map places broad landmarks and dates accounts/surveys while explicitly rejecting a measured route or precise speed. Research gaps to preserve: exact ignition, complete casualty count, missing servant voices, and the difference between evidence of spread and allegations of deliberate cause.
